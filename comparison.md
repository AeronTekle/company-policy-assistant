I compared three methods on the same ten questions, producing 30 reviewed answers from the 98-policy database. The questions covered vacation, remote work, expenses, passwords, travel receipts, working abroad, two leave policies in one question, missing pension information, a fictional benefit and an unspecified sick-leave allowance. Full context and RAG each answered all ten questions correctly. Rules-based search was fully correct once, partly correct eight times and incorrect once under a rubric that penalised irrelevant policies and incomplete answers. No unsupported factual claims were observed in the 30 answers; returning irrelevant existing text was treated separately from inventing facts. This small, selected sample does not establish a general hallucination rate. Full context averaged 4,610.9 LLM tokens per answer, compared with 408.4 for RAG and zero for rules.

For this small database, I would choose full context: it matched RAG's observed accuracy, requires no vector-index setup and was faster on average in this evaluation. Mean response times were 3.2355 seconds for full context and 4.0458 seconds for RAG; medians were 1.1949 and 1.5529 seconds. Rules averaged 0.0009 seconds but often returned irrelevant information. RAG used about 91.1% fewer LLM tokens and would be worth considering as the database grows, although that saving excludes embeddings. Initial index preparation took 45.1158 seconds, and embedding token counts were not reported. Each question was measured once per method, across two browser sessions, with a fixed method order; timing varies and is not a controlled benchmark. The Slack demonstration uses full context and cites the relevant policy.
